Stage 5 & 6

Fig. 4 — Feature Selection & Feature Engineering

Derivation of temporal, autoregressive lag, and rolling features strictly avoiding future target leakage.

Fig. 4 — Feature Selection & Engineering

📅 Calendar / Temporal Features

  • day_of_week 0 (Mon) to 6 (Sun)
  • month 1 to 12 (Seasonal cycle)
  • day_of_month 1 to 31 (Monthly pay/clinic days)
  • is_weekend Binary flag (Saturday/Sunday)

⏱️ Autoregressive Lag Features

  • lag_1_occupied_beds Occupied beds on day t-1
  • lag_1_occupancy_rate Occupied / Total on day t-1
  • recent_admissions New admissions on day t-1
  • recent_discharges Discharges on day t-1

📈 Rolling Trend Features

  • rolling_7_occupied_beds 7-day moving avg (shifted t-1)
  • rolling_7_admissions 7-day admission momentum
  • rolling_7_discharges 7-day discharge momentum
  • total_beds Current available ward capacity
Guaranteed Zero Lookahead Bias: All lag and rolling features are calculated using .shift(1) within distinct ward groups. Day $t$ target occupancy is strictly excluded from feature computation, ensuring the model only uses historical information that would realistically be available to hospital administrators at forecast time.

Why Chronological Splitting is Mandatory for Time Series: Random shuffling of hospital census observations would leak future seasonal patterns and trend slopes into past training observations, artificially inflating evaluation scores. A chronological split (earlier 80% for training, later 20% for testing) faithfully simulates real-world forward deployment.

Project Synthesis

Fig. 9 — Final Application Architecture & Viva Voce Guide

Essential machine learning principles and questions for college laboratory examination.

Fig. 9 — Final Overview & Viva Q&A
Q1: Why is this formulated as Supervised Regression?
The target variable (occupied_beds) is a continuous numerical count learned from labeled historical feature pairs (capacity, admissions, lag occupancy, and calendar variables).
Q2: Why choose Random Forest Regression?
Random Forest is an ensemble of decision trees using bagging (bootstrap aggregation). It captures non-linear clinical flow dynamics, resists overfitting, and handles correlated features without strict normality assumptions.
Q3: How is Target Data Leakage prevented?
All lag and rolling variables are shifted by 1 day (t-1). Future admissions, discharges, or target values on day t are never used as model inputs during training or prediction.
Q4: Why not randomly shuffle train/test sets?
Time-series observations possess temporal autocorrelation. Random shuffling would let future patterns bleed into training, resulting in falsely optimistic evaluation metrics.
Q5: What does an R² of 0.9833 mean?
It means the trained Random Forest explains 98.33% of the total variance in hospital occupied beds relative to a baseline mean-predicting model on the held-out test timeframe.
Q6: What is the clinical significance of the 85% BOR threshold?
Healthcare systems like the UK NHS and WHO identify 85% Bed Occupancy Rate as the critical threshold beyond which patient flow bottlenecking, ER boarding, and infection risks increase rapidly.
